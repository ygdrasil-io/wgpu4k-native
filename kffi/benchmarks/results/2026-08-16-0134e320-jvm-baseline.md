Reading — **the P4 dispatch improvement is real but modest (~1-3 ns/call), because the ~70 ns
per-call floor is dominated by the native trampoline/FFM round trip that allocation cost cannot
move:**

- **fire_1000 / fire_1000_no_routing (batched axes — the primary confirmation):** -2.1%..-4.4%
  across both runs (primary CI disjoint from P3; the re-run's fire_1000 CI only touches P3's
  lower edge — borderline but still below P3's point estimate). Per-call cost drops from
  ~72.7/71.1 ns to ~69.6-71.2/68.0-68.6 ns. Batching accumulates the per-dispatch savings,
  which is exactly why the batched axes are the reliable signal at this depth.
- **fire_one_no_routing:** -2.8% on both runs (74.55/74.59 vs 76.70), CIs disjoint — the
  no-userdata path lost its per-transition snapshot allocations (plan note: it had no map, but
  still the snapshots) and now shows the cleanest single-shot gain (~2.2 ns).
- **fire_one (routing + userdata):** -0.9%..-1.2% (75.92/76.12 vs 76.84) — directionally
  consistent across both runs but within CI overlap of P3. At ~1 ns the gain sits at the
  resolution edge of the single-shot axis. **Honest verdict: the single-shot routing axis cannot
  claim significance; the P4 goal "significativement sous 76.84" is not demonstrated by this axis
  alone — it is demonstrated by the batched axes.**
- **New routing/no-routing gap:** P3 measured them equal (~0.1 ns apart); P4 shows a consistent
  ~1.4-1.6 ns gap. The routing path retains token-table lookup + userdata dereference per
  dispatch; the no-routing path is a direct slot. The gap is now measurable because the
  per-dispatch snapshot allocation + CAS-object transition (equal on both paths, hence masked
  in the P3 comparison) are gone.
- **Attribution (why only ~1-3 ns/call):** at P3 the `DeliverySnapshot` was published through
  `snapshotRef.compareAndSet(...)` into a heap `AtomicReference` — C2 **cannot** scalar-replace
  CAS-published objects, so the per-transition snapshot allocation was real (TLAB bump,
  ~0.5-1.5 ns each); the local `AcquiredDelivery` was the only escape-eliminable allocation.
  The real P4 win is therefore (a) removing the per-dispatch TLAB-bump allocations and
  (b) replacing the CAS-object transition with a single `AtomicLong` CAS (cheaper atomic op).
  The measured ~1-3 ns/call is small because the ~70 ns floor is dominated by the native
  trampoline/FFM round trip, not by allocation. **For the native target (P5 re-baseline) the win
  should be LARGER:** Kotlin/Native has no JIT escape analysis, allocation is far costlier there,
  and the trampoline floor is much lower — the same removal should show a bigger Δ.

## Other axes stability (must be ±8%)

21 of 22 non-upcall scenarios are within the ±8% band (25 of 26 overall; the 4 upcall axes are
the P4 target, analyzed separately). Worst in-band non-upcall: `downcall.struct_by_value_arg`
+6.2%. 20 of 22 non-upcall scenarios have CIs overlapping P3; the two non-overlapping ones:

- **downcall.empty: +3.6%** — well inside the ±8% band; the CI non-overlap comes from P3's
  exceptionally tight CI (±0.03 ns), not from a large shift. No P4 mechanism (P4 touches only
  `CallbackRuntime`).
- **bakeoff.fmmExactDereferencedLookup: +10.9% in the full run (397.95 ± 24.84) —
  resolved by the focused re-run (363.98 ± 11.01, +1.5%, in band).** This axis is pure JDK FFM
  (per-call `Linker.nativeLinker().downcallHandle` construction + symbol lookup + cold dispatch;
  no kffi code on the path — see `DowncallEngineBakeoff.fmmExactDereferencedLookup`), so P4 has
  no mechanism to affect it. The full run's fork 1 was systematically inflated (387-432 ns vs
  375-394 fork 2; P3's fork spread was 354-363) — attributed to machine noise early in the run.
  Classified as measurement noise with re-run evidence, not a regression.

The remaining axes drift +0.2%..+6.2% (worst: `downcall.struct_by_value_arg` +6.2%,
`marshaling.scalarSafe` +4.6%, `downcall.struct_by_value_return` +4.7%, `marshaling.array_f64_1024`
+4.3%) — same direction and scale as the P3-vs-P2 drift; all CI-overlap. No P4 mechanism touches
downcall/marshaling/arena (P4 changes are confined to `CallbackRuntime`).

## Zero-allocation attribution note

The P4 goal is *structural*, and the measurement confirms it rather than proving it:

- **Source audit (M3.2 criterion):** `rg "DeliverySnapshot|AcquiredDelivery|\.toMap\(|emptyMap\("
  kffi/src/commonMain/kotlin/org/graphiks/kffi/CallbackRuntime.kt` → zero matches. Residual
  allocations (`NoUserdataSnapshot`, `RegistryEntry`, `PreparedCallbackRegistration`) sit on
  register/prepare/retire paths, off the per-dispatch hot path.
- **Bytecode inspection (this report):** compiled `CallbackRuntime.dispatchSafely` contains
  **zero `new` instructions**; `route` contains exactly 3 — all `new IllegalArgumentException`
  on failure paths (unrouted/closed). The success-path dispatch
  `dispatchSafely → route → tryEnter → invoke → leave` is allocation-free in bytecode; token
  lookup is an `AtomicReferenceArray` load (`TokenIndexTable.get`, no allocation). Reproducible
  with `javap -c -p kffi/build/classes/kotlin/jvm/main/org/graphiks/kffi/CallbackRuntime.class`
  (count `new` instructions in `dispatchSafely` and `route`).
- **Measurement:** consistent -1..-4% on the upcall axes (see above). The modest magnitude is
  the expected signature of removing TLAB-bump allocation + a CAS-object → CAS-primitive
  transition on top of a ~70 ns FFM-dominated floor (attribution detailed in the Upcall focus
  section — the P3 snapshots were CAS-published, not scalar-replaceable); the batched axes
  confirm the per-dispatch gain.
